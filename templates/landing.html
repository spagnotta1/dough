Built to be trusted with the real thing

Dough asks for a connection to your bank. Here is exactly what it does with it — every line below is stated the same way in the privacy policy.

Bank credentials never stored

You sign in on Plaid's own interface. Dough never sees, receives or stores your banking username or password.

Read-only connections

The access token Dough holds permits reading balances and history. There is no code path in this product that moves money.

Encrypted at rest

Institution tokens are encrypted with an authenticated cipher, so a copy of the database is not a usable credential without the key.

Households stay separate

Isolation is enforced at the query layer, not page by page — so a new screen cannot forget to apply it.

Your data belongs to you

Export everything as JSON, or delete your account outright, from your own settings page. No email required to get it back.

Never sold, never advertised against

No advertising trackers, no sale of personal information, no marketing email. The product is the product.

Security

What happens when you connect an account

Three steps, and the credential never passes through Dough in any of them.

  1. 1

    You authenticate with your bank

    The sign-in happens on Plaid's interface, against your institution. Dough is not in the middle of it and never receives what you typed.

  2. 2

    Dough receives a read-only token

    What comes back permits reading balances, holdings and history — nothing else. It is encrypted at rest with an authenticated cipher.

  3. 3

    You can revoke it in one click

    Disconnecting an institution deletes the stored token. Deleting your account removes your details, sessions, API tokens and connections.

Passwords

Stored only as a scrypt hash. Changing yours invalidates every existing session and API token at once.

Sessions

Signed, expiring, and cleared past their absolute lifetime — a stale browser is treated as a stranger, not as you.

Isolation

Household scoping is applied in the query layer with an ORM backstop, so a query that forgets it raises instead of leaking.

Logging

Passwords, tokens, account numbers, prompts and completions are excluded at the formatter, not per call site.

Audit trail

Sign-in, sign-out, invitations, token issue and revocation are recorded append-only, and record events rather than balances.

What we don't claim

No system is perfectly secure, and Dough does not pretend otherwise. The policy says the same thing in the same words.

Questions

The things people ask before connecting a bank

How does Dough connect to my bank?

Through Plaid. You authenticate on Plaid's own interface, against your institution, and Dough receives an access token in return — never your banking username or password. Dough uses that token to read account names and types, balances, and transaction history.

Can Dough move my money?

No. The connection is read-only and there is no payment, transfer or trading code path in the product at all. Dough can tell you a bill is due; it cannot pay it.

Is my information secure?

Passwords are stored as a scrypt hash. Institution tokens are encrypted at rest with an authenticated cipher, so a copy of the database yields nothing usable without the separate key. Sessions expire, and changing your password invalidates every session and API token you have.

No system is perfectly secure, and Dough does not claim otherwise. The privacy policy spells out the rest.

Which banks are supported?

Whichever ones Plaid supports — thousands of banks, credit unions and brokerages across the US, Canada and Europe. Anything it does not reach, you can import from a CSV statement, and the same categorization and budgeting applies to it.

Can I disconnect an account?

At any time, from your connections page. Disconnecting deletes the stored access token. You can also export everything Dough holds as a JSON file, or delete your account outright, from settings.

Does Dough sell my data?

No. There are no advertising trackers, no sale of personal information, and no sharing with third parties for their own marketing. When you use an AI feature, a summary of the relevant figures is sent to Anthropic to answer that question; your prompts and its replies are not written to logs or the audit trail.

Who else can see my figures?

Only the people you invite into your household. That is what a household is for, and it is also the boundary — no household can read another's data, which is enforced at the database query layer rather than page by page.
