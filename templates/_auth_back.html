{# "← Back to home" — the secondary way out of a signed-out form. The branding link covers the person who knows a logo is clickable. This is for everybody else, and it is what stops these pages being dead ends: before it, `/login` offered a password reset and sometimes a registration link, and `/join` with a broken invitation offered nothing at all. Rendered *outside* the card wherever there is one. Everything inside the card is about the form; this is about leaving, and putting it there would make it one more thing to do with the form. Outside also keeps it clear of the flash banners, which stack at the top of the card — the message saying why you are here and the way back out never contend for the same space. Kept quiet on purpose: no fill, no border, .78rem. The submit button is the only filled control on these pages and has to stay that way. ## Where this belongs, and where it does not Not on `/reset-password`. The link that got somebody there is spent by the time the form renders (see the docstring on `auth.reset_password`), so leaving means asking for a new email — an inviting exit on that page is a trap, and the page's own copy already says what to do. The branding stays clickable there because a logo is not an invitation to leave; this is. Not on `/setup` either: `/` redirects back to `/setup` while no account exists, so the link would be a loop. Not on the notice branches that already carry an `auth-cta` — a closed instance, a spent reset link, a confirmed address. A single-action page is clearer for having one action, and every one of those actions leads somewhere that does offer this. #}
